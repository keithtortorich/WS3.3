# WebStaffr: Investor Communication Guide

**Status:** Reference document, not investor-facing. Written 2026-07-24. Source-aligned with `GROWTH_AND_RETENTION_STRATEGY.md`'s conservative churn and CAC framing and `STRATEGY.md`'s reconciliation history. See `docs/outreach/Investor_Email_Draft.md` for the current draft this guide is meant to inform.

---

## 1. Core Principles

Transparency first: explicitly flag assumptions, risks, and unproven elements, CAC, churn, and growth ramps foremost among them. Investors at this stage generally prefer honest updates over rosy projections that later disappoint.

Data over narrative: prioritize real metrics from trials as they arrive, and lean on "illustrative" language for anything still modeled rather than measured.

Frequency and predictability: a regular cadence builds confidence more than any single update does. Set the cadence expectation upfront rather than letting it drift.

Action-oriented: every update should include a clear next step and a concrete way the investor can help, whether that is an introduction or a specific piece of advice.

Brevity: keep updates scannable. Short paragraphs, a few bullets, and a subject line that signals real content rather than vague progress.

Tone: confident but humble. "We're de-risking aggressively" reads better, and ages better, than "everything is going great."

---

## 2. Recommended Cadence and Content Types

Monthly updates, or immediately after a real milestone: progress on trials, real metrics as they exist, blockers, and any adjustment to the plan.

Quarterly deep dives: a full model review, real churn and CAC data as it accumulates, and integration status.

Ad hoc updates: major wins such as a first paying customer or a live integration, or material changes such as a corrected pricing figure or a revised growth assumption.

Before a major raise or ask: a detailed memo followed by a call, not an email alone.

---

## 3. Update Structure

**Subject line:** specific and grounded in real content, not vague progress language. For example, "WebStaffr Update: First Cohort Metrics and a Conservative Model Refresh," not "Update on WebStaffr."

**Opening:** warm and purpose stated plainly. A short line of thanks followed directly by what this update covers.

**Body:**

Wins and progress, two to four bullets, each tied to something specific and real.

Key metrics: trial count, conversion rate, early churn, and CAC, wherever real numbers exist rather than modeled ones.

Challenges and adjustments, framed as responsible conservatism rather than bad news. An example specific to this project: moving the internal churn planning assumption to 8% is a strength to state plainly, not something to soften.

Integration and product status, stated as what is actually true right now, not what is planned.

Financial and model notes, always carrying the "illustrative, not a forecast" caveat wherever a modeled number appears.

**Forward look:** the next concrete milestone with a real date if one exists, and a specific ask for how the investor can help.

**Close:** grateful, with a clear call to action, such as offering a short call.

---

## 4. Practices Specific to WebStaffr

Always caveat optimism. Wherever a deck number appears, such as 6% churn or an organic-channel CAC figure, remind the reader these are best case, illustrative, and unproven past the first cohort, consistent with `GROWTH_AND_RETENTION_STRATEGY.md` Section 3.

Default to the 8% churn planning rate in any communication, not the 5 to 6% aspirational figures, per the same document.

Attach simple visuals rather than dense spreadsheets: a churn sensitivity chart or a cohort retention curve reads faster than a table of numbers.

Present the ServiceTitan integration honestly as what it is today: a code complete, test covered, read first package against the V2 API, not yet live against a real tenant, moving toward a live webhook integration as the near term milestone. See `GROWTH_AND_RETENTION_STRATEGY.md` Section 5 and `docs/outreach/servicetitan-partner-application.md` for the current accurate status.

Present retention protocols as scaffolding being prepared for when trial data justifies them, not as an operating program that already exists, per `GROWTH_AND_RETENTION_STRATEGY.md` Section 4.

Deliver bad news early. If a real trial cohort shows higher churn or slower conversion than modeled, say so promptly along with the mitigation already underway, rather than waiting for a scheduled update.

Lean into founder domain credibility where it is genuinely relevant, without overstating what is proven versus modeled.

---

## 5. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Over-anchoring on the deck's more optimistic numbers without restating the caveats attached to them.

Presenting forward-looking retention templates or integration plans as though they are current reality.

Vague updates with no metric attached, such as "making good progress" with nothing to substantiate it.

Radio silence during a quiet period. Even a short "still validating, here is what we are testing" note is better than no update at all.

---

## 6. Assets Worth Preparing

A one-page investor update template, reusable each cycle rather than rebuilt from scratch.

A conservative model, kept in a spreadsheet with clearly separated tabs for the baseline case and the sensitivity cases, mirroring the churn sensitivity table in `GROWTH_AND_RETENTION_STRATEGY.md` Section 3.

A short FAQ document covering the questions likely to recur: the churn planning assumption, the CAC caveat, and the timing logic behind the Tampa and multi-city expansion scenarios.

Any of the three above can be built as an actual file on request; this guide describes what they should contain, not a substitute for having them.
