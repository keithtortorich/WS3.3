# Brand Governance Unifier

## When to Use This Skill

You should invoke this skill whenever governance consolidation, brand standardization, or multi-document coherence is the goal. Common scenarios:

- **Consolidation**: "Here are 5 brand docs. Make them one coherent system."
- **Reconciliation**: "Our style guide contradicts our marketing positioning. Fix it."
- **Audit**: "Is our new customer-facing copy on-brand?"
- **Enforcement**: "Bring this copy into line with our governance standards."
- **Assessment**: "Do these docs read like they came from the same source?"

---

## The Two-Phase Process

### Phase 1: Analysis & Strategic Planning

Before rewriting anything, you'll:

1. **Read every supplied document completely** and identify:
   - Core philosophy and brand identity
   - Brand positioning (visual, verbal, strategic)
   - Design and communication philosophies
   - Sales doctrine and strategic principles
   - Contradictions, redundancies, and gaps

2. **Establish Governing Principles** that will underpin everything:
   - The company's core identity
   - The "complexity principle" (absorb complexity inside, offer clarity outside)
   - Executive vs. Commercial communication standards
   - Brand positioning rules (visual identity, tone, vocabulary)
   - Decision-making philosophy
   - Cross-document consistency rules

3. **Define Document Architecture** before rewriting:
   - Determine the unique role of each document
   - Map dependencies and reinforcement points
   - Identify what should be consolidated vs. kept separate

### Phase 2: Production & Standardization

Once the plan is set:

1. **Rewrite using the governing principles**
   - Do NOT preserve wording—preserve philosophy
   - Improve organization (use tables, charts, visual structures)
   - Remove contradictions, repetition, and ambiguity
   - Increase clarity and authority

2. **Maintain Two Communication Styles Consistently**

   **Executive** (leadership, investors, strategy, internal governance):
   - Elevated vocabulary
   - Precise language
   - Calm authority
   - Executive polish
   - Accuracy before persuasion

   **Commercial** (customers, marketing, websites, sales):
   - Grade 8 reading level
   - Simple language
   - Outcome-focused
   - High readability (large font, ample spacing, concise copy)
   - Tradesperson-friendly
   - Premium metaphors without sacrificing clarity

3. **Ensure All Docs Reinforce the Governing Philosophy**
   - Customers buy outcomes, not technology
   - [Company] is an operational [benefit] platform, not an AI feature company (or equivalent)
   - Complexity belongs inside the company, never between company and customer
   - Clarity is a competitive advantage
   - Premium organizations communicate with confidence, restraint, consistency
   - Every sentence supports trust, operational excellence, and measurable value

## Brand Identity Considerations

If the company has specific brand requirements (color codes, font families, formatting rules), these should be:
- **Documented explicitly** in the design governance
- **Applied consistently** across all outputs
- **Never violated** for readability or expedience

Example structure for brand rules:
- Logo treatment (sizing, spacing, color variations)
- Primary/secondary typefaces and sizing
- Color palette with hex/RGB values and usage rules
- Typography hierarchy
- Spacing and layout principles

## When Consolidating Multiple Documents

1. **Read all documents end-to-end first** before writing anything
2. **Create a single source-of-truth architecture** that eliminates redundancy
3. **Route related content to the right document** (e.g., font rules → design governance; tone examples → editorial guidelines)
4. **Cross-reference, don't duplicate** when concepts span documents
5. **Use tables and visual structures** to show relationships and rules clearly

## Example: Typical Output Structure

A unified governance system might include:

1. **Visual & Design Governance** - Colors, fonts, logo, layout, spacing, component patterns
2. **Editorial & Writing Governance** - Tone, voice, vocabulary rules, examples for each style
3. **Sales & Positioning Doctrine** - Value positioning, customer problem statements, objection handling
4. **Strategic Positioning** - Market positioning, competitive differentiation, core messaging
5. **Customer-Facing Standards** - Marketing copy templates, guardrails for commercial voice

Each document is focused and self-contained, but together they form one system.

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Preserving outdated wording** from legacy docs (philosophy yes, phrasing no)
- **Treating style guides as prescriptive** rather than principled (explain the why, not just the rule)
- **Allowing redundancy** between documents (consolidate, don't repeat)
- **Contradicting yourself across docs** (resolve conflicts during Phase 1, not after)
- **Using em dashes or other flagged punctuation** (check company-specific rules)
- **Referencing old iterations** in the new governance (start fresh, don't apologize)
